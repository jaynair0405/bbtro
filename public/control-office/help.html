Help & How-To

Everything the Control Office does on this platform, written as tasks: find the job you need, follow the steps. Each task names the page it lives on.

Use Print (top right) for a paper copy — it prints the whole guide, including the answers in the last section.

How the system thinks — worth two minutes

A train and its working are two different things

This one idea explains most of the surprises, so it is worth reading before anything else.

  • A train is the train itself — its number, name, running days, coach type. It lives in Settings → Trains.
  • A working (also called the loco link) is that train's appearance on one sheet, with the loco you expect for it — shed, loco class, HOG, link attribute. It lives in Settings → Loco Link and Coach Types.

The daily sheets show workings, not trains. A train can exist in the system and appear on no sheet at all — that is normal, not a fault. Vande Bharat trains are the clearest example: they are self-propelled and need no loco, so they have no working and never appear on a sheet.

If you want a train to appear on a sheet, it needs a loco link (a sheet and a time). Editing the train alone will not put it there.

The nine sheets

Work is divided by terminal and direction: CSMT-UP / CSMT-DN, LTT-UP / LTT-DN, KR-UP / KR-DN, PNVL-UP / PNVL-DN, and Bypass for trains that avoid the Mumbai terminals and run via Vasai Road.

DN = leaving Mumbai. UP = arriving. On a DN sheet you record the loco that will take the train out; on an UP sheet, the loco that brought it in and where it goes next.

Daily work

1 · Fill the daily sheet

Dashboard → the sheet you want (e.g. CSMT-DN)

  1. Pick the date — Today and Tomorrow are one click; any other date from the date box.
  2. Trains are listed in time order, so you can work down the sheet as the day goes.
  3. Type the loco number in the loco no box on the train's row.
  4. Fill what applies: the train the loco came in on (or goes out on), remarks, maintenance schedule and due date.
  5. Press Save on the row.

Not sure which locos are free? Press Show available locos to list what is standing at that terminal.

You can edit today, tomorrow, and the last 7 days. The window is deliberately wide because actuals are often filled in over the following days. Older dates are locked.

2 · Find a train or loco quickly

Any daily sheet — the search box at the top

Type a train number, loco number or train name. Matching rows are highlighted as you type, so you don't have to scroll a long sheet hunting for one entry.

3 · Record a second loco, push-pull or an assist

Daily sheet — the second box on the row

  1. Enter the main loco first and save.
  2. Put the second loco in the 2nd loco box (it reads rear loco on a push-pull working).
  3. Choose what the second loco is doing — rear, coupler, assist, or dead in tow.
Rear is only for push-pull trains. On an ordinary train the system will refuse it — use coupler or assist.

If the assist or coupler loco is to work a DN train later, type that train in the box beside it; the system will carry the loco onto that train's row for you.

4 · A loco that failed

Daily sheet — row options

  • Tick Failed within our division so it is counted against the division.
  • If the failed loco is left attached and towed, record the new working loco in front and the failed one as dead in tow — both correctly leave with the train.
  • If the loco is being taken out of service, also mark it sick (task 7).

5 · Assign locos with the Assignment Board

Dashboard → Loco Assignment Board

The board is the daily sheet turned around: locos standing at a terminal on one side, the trains still needing one on the other. Useful when you want to place the locos you have rather than work train by train.

  1. Choose the terminal and date.
  2. Needs loco lists the workings still unfilled. Each shows what it wants, e.g. wants AQE · HOG · WAP7 · LHB — expected shed, HOG requirement, loco class, coach type.
  3. Standing at … lists the locos available, best fit first, with the reason shown (✓ fits, or MISLINK / NO HOG).
  4. Pick a loco for a working. It saves immediately and the board refreshes — there is no separate confirm button.

If a loco has to be changed late, the board offers three choices rather than an error: replace it, attach it with the old one dead in tow, or add it as a second loco.

The board and the sheet write the same records. Whichever you use, the other shows the result.

6 · Print the day

Daily sheet, or Dashboard → Consolidated Sheet

  • Print / Save this sheet as PDF — the sheet you are on.
  • Open all sheets in one PDF — every sheet for that date in one document.

Locos

7 · Mark a loco sick

Dashboard → Sick Loco Mgmt (or Mark Sick on Loco Availability)

  1. Add a row and enter the loco number.
  2. Record the defect or reason, the date and time it became ineffective, and where it happened.
  3. Add the shed arrival date and time once it reaches the shed.
  4. Save.

The sheet groups locos into COG (coaching) and GOODS blocks, with separate blocks for diesels. The system works out which block from the loco class and the train it was working; if it guesses wrong, set the category on the row and your choice wins.

8 · Mark a loco fit again

Sick Loco Mgmt

  1. Find the loco in its block.
  2. Enter the date it was passed fit, then Confirm Ready.
  3. If it was handed over to another shed or railway instead of being repaired, record it as handed over (H/O) with that date.

The loco then leaves the live sick sheet and stays in the Sick Loco Report history (task 26) with the number of days it was out.

9 · Record a defect

Dashboard → Defect Reports

  1. Press to add a defect.
  2. Enter the Loco No, Working Date and a Description — these are required.
  3. Set category, severity, status, terminal and train number where known.
  4. Save Defect.

A defect is a fault worth recording against a loco; marking a loco sick (task 7) is what takes it out of service. A loco can have defects without being sick.

10 · Add, move or remove a loco in Availability

Dashboard → Loco Availability

  • Add Loco — record a loco as standing at a terminal (one that arrived light, or from outside the division).
  • Move Loco → Move to another terminal — when a loco is moved light.
  • Remove from availability — when it has left the division and no sheet entry covers it.
  • Mark Sick — jumps straight to the sick entry for that loco.
If the list looks too long, read “Why does Availability show locos that aren't there?” before clearing anything by hand.

11 · Check HOG position

Dashboard → HOG Position

Shows which workings need a HOG-capable loco and whether one is placed, so a train that needs hotel load isn't left with a loco that cannot supply it.

12 · Check schedules falling due

Dashboard → Loco Schedule Due

Lists locos by maintenance schedule and due date, overdue ones flagged. The schedule type and due date come from what is entered on the daily sheet, so keeping that column filled is what keeps this page useful.

13 · Manage the loco master

Dashboard → Loco Management · needs admin rights

This is the roster of locos themselves — not where they are today. Use it to:

  • search a loco by number and see or edit its class, shed, zone, traction and HOG fitment;
  • add a loco (diesels are added here as they come in);
  • transfer a loco to another shed or zone — the change is recorded with a reason;
  • change status to Transferred Out or Condemned;
  • see a loco's history of transfers and status changes.
Don't confuse this with Availability (task 10). Availability is where a loco is standing today; this is the loco's permanent record.

Trains and settings

Everything in this group is under Dashboard → Settings and needs admin rights.

14 · Add a special train

Settings → Scheduled Specials → Add

  1. Enter the train number and name.
  2. Choose the sheet it runs on (e.g. CSMT-DN) and its direction.
  3. Enter the event time — the time at the station where the loco link happens. On a DN sheet that is the terminal; on an UP sheet it is the takeover point (LNL, IGP or ROHA).
  4. Set Effective From and, if known, Effective Until. Leave Until blank if it runs until further notice.
  5. Set the running days, the expected shed, loco type, coach type, HOG and push-pull as applicable.
  6. Save.

The special now appears on that sheet, on its running days, between those dates only.

15 · Stop a special from running

Settings → Scheduled Specials → Close on the row

  1. Find the special.
  2. Press Close and enter the last date it ran.

It disappears from the sheet from the next day onward, and sheets for earlier dates still show it with whatever locos were recorded — the history is not lost.

Do not use this on a train that has merely stopped for a few days; use Skip dates (task 17) instead, so you don't have to re-create it.

16 · Extend a special that is continuing

Settings → Scheduled Specials → Extend

Enter the new end date. Use this when a special is prolonged rather than creating a second entry — a duplicate would put the train on the sheet twice.

17 · Skip particular dates

Settings → Scheduled Specials → Skip

Enter a date the train will not run. The train stays on the books and keeps its dates, but that day's sheet leaves it out. Use it for cancellations and blocks.

18 · Change a train's running days

Settings → Trains → find the train → Edit

  1. Search the train number and press edit.
  2. Tick the days it runs — Mon to Sun — or set it to daily.
  3. Save.
Saving here also updates the train's workings, so the sheets follow immediately. This is why running days should be changed here rather than on individual links — a seasonal curtailment set in the wrong place never reaches the sheet.

19 · Change the coach type

Settings → Trains → find the train → Edit → Coach Type

  1. Search the train and press edit.
  2. Set Coach Type — it sits with the train's own details (Type, Direction, Traction), near the top of the form, not in the Loco Link section below.
  3. Save.

The coach type then shows on the daily sheet and on the Assignment Board, so whoever places a loco can see what stock it is for.

Coach type belongs to the train, which is why it works for a Vande Bharat that has no loco link at all. If a particular working genuinely runs different stock, that one link can be set separately under Loco Link and Coach Types.

20 · Change the loco link — shed, railway and link attribute

Settings → Loco Link and Coach Types

  1. Filter by Sheet or Shed, or search the train number or name.
  2. Press edit on the working you want.
  3. Change what the link expects: Shed, Loco Type, Link Attribute, Coach Type, HOG, Push-Pull, event time and section.
  4. Save.

This is what the Assignment Board checks a loco against — change the shed here and a loco from a different shed will be flagged as a mislink, and appear in the Mis-link Report (task 27).

Use Add Link on this tab to put an existing train onto a sheet it is not yet on.

21 · Renumber a train

Settings → Trains → Renumber

Enter the new number and the date it changed. Use this rather than editing the number by hand: the old number stays searchable, past records stay attached to the same train, and the sheets show a “previously numbered …” note so nobody is confused by the change.

22 · Add a regular train, or take one off

Settings → Trains

To add: press add, fill number, name, type, direction, from and to stations, running days and coach type. If it is to appear on a sheet, also give the sheet and event time in the Loco Link section — without those it is recorded but appears nowhere.

To take one off: press the delete icon and enter the last date it ran. As with a special, it leaves the sheet from the next day and earlier sheets keep their history.

Timetable

23 · Look up a train's timings

Dashboard → Working Time Table → Train lookup

Type the train number and press Find. You get the train's details and every halt with arrival and departure. A +1 beside a time means it reaches that station the next day. Old numbers work too — the page shows a “was …” note.

The WTT is open to all staff, not only the Control Office.

24 · See everything through a station

Working Time Table → Station board

Pick the station, direction and a time window, then Show trains. Useful for answering “what passes IGP between 2 and 4”. You can also compare several trains' full halt lists side by side.

25 · Print the full working timetable

Working Time Table → Full WTT

  1. Choose direction — All, Down or Up.
  2. Choose how many train columns per page (8 to 16; 10 is usual).
  3. Export PDF for the printed book, or Export Excel for a spreadsheet.

It prints in the classic form — trains as columns, stations as rows — grouped Down, then Up, then Bypass, then MEMU.

Print in landscape, and switch on Background graphics in the browser's print dialog so the shaded rows come out.

Reports

26 · Sick loco report

Dashboard → Sick Loco Report

  1. Choose a period — Last 7d, Last 30d, Last 90d, or your own From and To dates.
  2. Narrow by shed, or by status (still open / closed).
  3. Apply.

Results are split into COG and GOODS blocks, each with its own count, and the summary across the top gives totals, how many are still open, how many closed or handed over, and the average days out. Excel and PDF keep the same grouping.

27 · Mis-link report

Dashboard → Mis-link Reports

Lists workings where the loco placed does not match what the link expects, in three degrees: wrong railway, wrong shed, and wrong loco class. Use it to see where links are being worked by the wrong power.

28 · Consolidated sheet

Dashboard → Consolidated Sheet

All sheets for one date in a single view, for a full picture of the day or for printing.

Why does it do that?

Why does Loco Availability show locos that are not actually there?

Because the list is built from what has been entered, and a loco only leaves it when something records it leaving.

A loco is taken out of the available list when it appears on a DN link — front or rear — dated on or after the day it arrived. So if a loco left the division and no sheet entry covers it (worked light to a shed, transferred out, or the link was simply never filled), nothing tells the system it has gone and it keeps standing there.

What to do: fill the link that took it out, if there is one — that is the real fix and it corrects the count everywhere. If the loco genuinely left without a working, use Remove from availability on the Loco Availability page.

What not to do: clear the list wholesale. A loco that is booked on tomorrow's train is correctly still shown as standing here today.

A loco that has been standing for several days with no booking is the one worth questioning — that is usually a missing entry rather than a loco that is really idle.

I deactivated a train, but it still shows on the sheet

Two things can cause this.

The date. Stopping a train takes effect from the day after the last running date you entered. Sheets for that date and earlier still show it — deliberately, so the record of what actually ran is not rewritten.

The page is showing an old copy. Reload with Ctrl+Shift+R (Cmd+Shift+R on a Mac).

If it still appears on a future date after reloading, the train most likely has more than one working — check Settings → Loco Link and Coach Types and search its number: a train on two sheets has two links, and each has to be closed.

It says “Sheet and Event Time are required” when I am only changing something small

You have filled in something in the Loco Link section of the train form, and a link cannot be created without a sheet and a time. The system is asking you to finish the link, not blocking your edit.

If you did not mean to create a link, clear the link fields and save again. If you did, give it the sheet and the event time.

Note that Coach Type is not part of the link section — it sits with the train's own details, so changing it never asks for this.

Why do some trains show no coach type?

Because nobody has set one. Existing values were filled in automatically where the train's workings already agreed on a coach type; where a train's workings disagreed, it was left blank rather than guessing.

Set it on the train (task 19) and it applies everywhere that train appears.

Why is a Dadar loco shown at VVH?

Dadar terminates trains but has no stabling shed, so locos run light to VVH and back to attach. Arrivals at Dadar are therefore positioned at VVH, and Dadar-originating workings are listed on the VVH board.

The two are treated as one pool — a loco actually held at Dadar still appears on the VVH board, badged AT DR, so it stays assignable.

Why can't I edit an older date?

Sheets stay open for today, tomorrow and the past 7 days. Beyond that they are locked so the record settles. If something genuinely needs correcting outside that window, it has to be done by the administrator.

The Working Time Table says “No WTT data”

The timings come from the timetable data loaded into the system, not from the daily sheets. If the page is blank for every train, the data has not been loaded or the service needs restarting after an update — report it rather than re-entering anything.

My change isn't showing

Reload the page with Ctrl+Shift+R (Cmd+Shift+R on a Mac). Browsers keep a copy of a page and will happily show you yesterday's version. This is the single most common cause of "it didn't save".

If a fresh reload still shows the old value, check you saved the row — the daily sheet saves per row, not per page.

Everything is greyed out / it says I may not make changes

Your login is a view-only one. Several accounts are set up to read the Control Office without changing it — including the SSE-HQ desk and supervisory logins. Nothing is wrong; ask the administrator if you need working access.

If something looks wrong

Before re-entering anything, try in this order: reload the page properly (Ctrl+Shift+R), check the date you are looking at, and check whether the train has more than one working. Most surprises are one of those three.

If a number looks wrong on a report, note the train or loco number and the date — that is what is needed to trace it.
